The market: own it or rent it

Public pages only, read on 23 November 2026, each with its link. No login, no contact, nothing copied. Prices are as each vendor's page showed them to a reader outside the UK, so most are in dollars; where a page did not say whether a price is monthly or annual billing, I say so. A page is not the product: what a page does not say may still exist. Builds on landscape.d's 31 players, read on 22 November.

What we do better, in one line each

  • The whole site leaves with you, design included, not only the words. Of the eleven above, only Lovable says the same, and it is a tool for people who code.
  • Paid once, against monthly prices that rise: Squarespace raised its annual plans by up to 26% this year (below).
  • A team builds it from your words and photos, and your own AI edits it inside a written policy.

What we do not do yet

  • Nothing is sold yet. The launch offer was decided on 23 Nov; the way to take it up (the hub's message box) is being switched on.
  • Editing needs a Claude plan, or a partner, so "no technical skill" is not yet true.
  • No shop, no bookings, no forms of our own: the sites are static files.
  • No support desk, no service level, no certification.

What a person charges Fact

Third-party estimates, not survey data, and no named agency's price list was found:

  • A five-page brochure site: freelancer £1,500 to £3,000, agency £3,000 to £5,000, excluding VAT; care plans £25 to £100 a month, agency retainers "typically £50 to £200" (Whito, page dated 18 Nov 2026, method not given).
  • Freelancer, three to five pages: £500 to £1,500; maintenance "£50-£300/month" (ExpertSure, "verified Oct 2026").

So the recorded £500 anchor sits at the low end of a freelancer's build, and well below an agency's. That is a fact about the market, not a price recommendation.

Renting gets dearer Fact

  • Squarespace raised annual-plan prices by up to 26% (Basic $16 to $19 a month, Core $23 to $29, Plus $39 to $49; US prices): PetaPixel, 17 Jul 2026.
  • Wix announced that "the price of some Premium plans" goes up for new subscriptions and renewals, with legacy plans moved to new tiers: Wix Help Centre, updated 19 Nov 2025. No new prices on that page.
  • The EU Data Act's switching rules apply from 12 Sep 2025; switching charges are capped at cost until 12 Jan 2027 and banned after it (DLA Piper, 31 Jul 2025; Pinsent Masons). Whether a website builder is a "data processing service" under the Act is open; I found no regulator view. A talking point, not a claim.

The channel: web people Fact

About 2,130 UK web design businesses, about £657.5m in 2025, an analyst's estimate (IBISWorld; its own pages disagree, one gives £623.2m for 2024). Agencies already resell white-label builders by the month (Duda above). Our offer to them is the opposite shape: the client owns the site, the agency keeps the care.

What this means Proposal

  1. Lead with leaving, not with building. Every rented builder read either says you cannot leave or lets only part of the site go. "Your whole site, yours to move" is a claim we can prove and they cannot make.
  2. The managed services prove people pay a team monthly ($25 to $125 a month at UENI, with a $599 setup). None hands over the site. Our offer to A and C is that team, with the site handed over.
  3. Agencies are already a channel for white-label builders. Ours is the inverse: the client owns, the agency keeps the care fee.
  4. Still to read: UK prices for Wix, Squarespace and Webflow from a UK browser; Netlify's terms on commercial use; Durable's terms on export.
