Sovereign Website: where we are

An honest inventory for Dinis, read between 12:49 and 13:10 UTC on 23 November 2026. Fact marks what I read, with where; Proposal marks what I suggest. The go-to-market plan is on its own page; the market research on another.

In five lines

  1. The machine works: in five days one organisation went from nothing to 66 repositories and 61 live addresses, each made by a pipeline under a written policy, with HTTPS, DNS and signed commits.
  2. The shop window is half dressed: 25 of those addresses are still a seed page or a stub of under 100 words.
  3. Nothing is sold. The price is three numbers, none decided. There is no way for a visitor to ask for a site except to be told how.
  4. The proof that exists is people's own sites: 11 on the hub, and one owner who took hers over and edits it herself.
  5. What Dinis wants to sell, the agentic workflow, is visible (agents, keys, policies, the operator page) but not yet packaged as something a buyer can ask for.

What stopped halfway Fact

  • 25 stub sites. Each is a promise on the hub's family menu that shows a placeholder. The platform sites (Odoo, OpenRouter, OWASP, Wikipedia) were the B to C to B traffic plan; four of the eight are still seeds.
  • No way in. The hub says there is no request form; sovereignwebsite.ai has prices but no "start" action a visitor can take alone.
  • No decided price. £500, £250 and £50 or €50 all appear, labelled; the market reads them as undecided (landscape.d, weakness 4).
  • One handover in eleven. Ten people's sites are still held by the organisation.
  • The owner still needs a Claude plan or a partner to edit. "No technical skill" is not yet true (landscape.d's report).
  • Partners: a first draft, none signed (partners page).
  • The strategy agents cannot sign in their own home. strategy.d (05:15 UTC) and I (12:55 UTC) were both refused access to the Factory, so neither key is on its dev branch and this site's signer check refuses our commits.
